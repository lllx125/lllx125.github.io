Overview

Astrobee XRP is Cornell Cup Robotics’ educational take on NASA’s free-flying robot. The team built a propulsion-based prototype around the Experiential Robotics Platform, with two impellers and twelve servo-operated vents. Opening different pairs of vents produces translation or rotation.

I developed the Unity simulation to explore that motion in zero gravity, modeling vent geometry, motor response, and forces on the robot. I also built a VR minigame for spatial visualization and STEM outreach, letting visitors fly the robot with handheld controllers.

Simulation

From vent opening to motion

Each vent has a position and thrust direction on the robot. The simulation converts its opening angle into an outlet area, accounts for impeller spin-up and the other open vents in the same plenum, then applies the resulting force at that vent’s position. Unity’s rigid-body physics turns those forces into movement and torque.

Input Vent opening Thrust Motion

Ideal and perturbed models

An optional mode adds Gaussian perturbations to mass, center of mass, inertia, vent positions, and force vectors. A comparison scene puts an ideal model alongside the perturbed robot so differences in their paths are visible.

Blue exhaust plumes scale with thrust, and the motor sound changes with force output. These cues make it easier to see which vents are active and how their combined output affects flight.

Inside Unity

VR experience

Fly with handheld controllers

The VR version uses Unity’s XR Interaction Toolkit. The left thumbstick controls two translation axes and the X/Y buttons control the third; the right thumbstick controls two rotation axes and A/B control the third. Those inputs open the same vent groups used by the desktop simulator.

Experiment and start again

Hold the left trigger to fill a progress bar and reset the robot. Hold the right trigger to spawn an object. Progress bars and audio give feedback while the trigger is held, supporting repeated experiments during outreach demonstrations.
