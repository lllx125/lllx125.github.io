Mini XRP

A smaller XRP with room to keep building. A 14.1 cm chassis carries the original electronics, motors, battery, and a centered drawing attachment, with snap-on rails for accessories.

Overview

Mini XRP is a compact redesign of the Experiential Robotics Platform, an educational robot for hands-on STEM learning. It reduces the standard 18.92 × 18.92 cm footprint to 14.1 × 14.1 cm while retaining the XRP board, two motors, battery, two caster balls, and pen-bot attachment.

I co-developed Mini XRP with Keegan Lewick in about a month, using CAD, 3D printing, and repeated fit tests to refine the mechanical system. We exhibited it at the FIRST Robotics Championship in Houston.

Design

A modular chassis

The rail around the chassis lets accessories clamp into hexagonal indents. Separate motor mounts and a central electronics core attach with hex bolts and clips. Matching A, B, and C labels guide assembly.

The battery sits centrally, while the XRP board slides into triangular slots and is retained by flexures. Separate clips secure the battery pack and make it accessible for replacement.

A pen at the center

The drawing attachment places its marker straight down through the center of the robot. Angled motor mounts route the wires away from that space, keeping the pen mechanism clear.

The design retains the XRP accessory system, and a separate LEGO-compatible version adds mounting interfaces for further customization.

Iterations

From one piece to separate mounts

An early 12 × 12 cm chassis integrated the motor mounts, but the motors had to sit at different angles and the print required extensive supports. Separating the mounts made printing more practical and allowed a symmetrical layout.

Making room to turn and draw

An octagonal iteration left too little clearance for the wheels and pen-bot. Shortening the corner edges created more room for wheel rotation while preserving space for the drawing attachment.

Printing and assembly

The basic parts are designed for PLA and a 0.4 mm nozzle, without support material. The documented basic set takes about five hours on a Bambu A1. Snap-fit components and removable clips support repeated assembly and disassembly.

User testing highlighted tight alignment pins and unclear small parts. Those comments pointed to larger fit tolerances and a short illustrated subassembly guide as useful refinements.
