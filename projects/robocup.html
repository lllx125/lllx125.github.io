Overview

Cornell Cup Robotics’ RoboCup project develops a soccer robot that can position itself, retain and direct a ball, and kick toward a goal. The Fall 2026 design integrates the frame and three motor-mount structures into one printed chassis assembly, with a front dribbler and roller, a solenoid holder, and removable mounts for the battery, controller, and AprilTag.

I led an accelerated soccer-robot build under a two-week deadline, coordinating about nine contributors across mechanical, electrical, and software subteams. I modularized mechanical interfaces and coordinated integration. The photo shows the physical prototype with its drivetrain and front roller installed; the CAD views document the Fall 2026 mechanical design.

Modular by design

Change one part at a time

The chassis is the common printed structure, combining the frame and three motor-mount structures. The solenoid holder, electronics carrier, marker holder, dribbler frame, and roller remain detachable, so those modules can be revised without replacing the chassis.

Snap bolts and hex clips provide removable connections. The detachable modules can be oriented independently for printing and inspected before assembly.

Interfaces drive the iteration

The same modularity makes fit and alignment important. The design needs clearance around wheels, a consistent ball-contact region, an unobstructed actuator stroke, and access to connectors and removable hardware.

The CAD uses distinct colors to make component boundaries visible. These colors identify parts in the model; they do not specify fabrication materials.

Mechanisms

Drivetrain

Three motor-mount structures are included in the chassis print. Each station locates an XRP motor and wheel assembly. After removing the custom supports, the repeated interfaces can be checked for motor seating, shaft and wheel clearance, and retention.

Ball handling and kicking

The front frame supports a replaceable roller, intended to draw the ball toward the robot and retain it during positioning. A separate holder locates the solenoid for the kicking mechanism, allowing its fit and stroke clearance to be adjusted independently.

Electronics and tracking

A removable carrier organizes the battery and XRP controller. The upper AprilTag holder provides a defined mounting surface for the tracking marker while keeping it separate from the main chassis.

Component views

Printing the chassis

Print upside down

The updated build instructions place the complete chassis assembly upside down, with its upper face toward the build plate. The motor-mount structures print with the frame.

Retain the custom support geometry built into the model and turn off slicer-generated supports. Remove the custom supports after printing, then check motor seating and the interfaces for the remaining modules.

Next checks

Further checks focus on motor seating, wheel clearance, removable-mount retention, roller rotation, ball contact, and access for servicing. The actuator, electronics, marker, and ball-handling modules should be checked against their neighboring parts during each revision.

The documentation now includes the physical prototype, complete chassis assembly, and print orientation. Detailed print parameters, final material choices, and measured dribbling, kicking, and tracking results remain to be recorded.
