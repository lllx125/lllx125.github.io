Overview

Cornell Cup Robotics’ RoboCup project develops a soccer robot that can position itself, retain and direct a ball, and kick toward a goal. The Fall 2026 mechanical design combines three motor-mount stations with a front dribbler and roller, a solenoid holder, and removable mounts for the battery, controller, and AprilTag.

I led an accelerated soccer-robot build under a two-week deadline, coordinating about nine contributors across mechanical, electrical, and software subteams. I modularized mechanical interfaces and coordinated integration. The CAD views here show the Fall 2026 mechanical design.

Modular by design

Change one part at a time

The chassis is the common base. Motor mounts, the solenoid holder, electronics carrier, marker holder, dribbler frame, and roller are separate parts, so a local design change can be tested without reprinting the whole robot.

Snap bolts and hex clips provide removable connections. Separating the modules also lets each part be oriented independently for printing and inspected before assembly.

Interfaces drive the iteration

The same modularity makes fit and alignment important. The design needs clearance around wheels, a consistent ball-contact region, an unobstructed actuator stroke, and access to connectors and removable hardware.

The CAD uses distinct colors to make component boundaries visible. These colors identify parts in the model; they do not specify fabrication materials.

Mechanisms

Drivetrain

Three motor-mount stations locate the drive units around the chassis. Each separate mount supports an XRP motor and wheel assembly, making motor fit, wheel clearance, and retention accessible for individual checks.

Ball handling and kicking

The front frame supports a replaceable roller, intended to draw the ball toward the robot and retain it during positioning. A separate holder locates the solenoid for the kicking mechanism, allowing its fit and stroke clearance to be adjusted independently.

Electronics and tracking

A removable carrier organizes the battery and XRP controller. The upper AprilTag holder provides a defined mounting surface for the tracking marker while keeping it separate from the main chassis.

Component views

Next checks

The next step for the documented design is to trial-fit the chassis and one motor station, then check the actuator, electronics, marker, and ball-handling modules against their neighboring parts. Key checks include wheel clearance, removable-mount retention, roller rotation, ball contact, and access for servicing.

The Fall 2026 documentation records the CAD design and proposed manufacturing workflow. Print settings, final materials, and measured dribbling, kicking, and tracking results remain to be recorded.
