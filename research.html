AtomicAndPhysicalConstants.jl: A Fast Julia Package to Access Particle Properties and Fundamental Constants

L. Li, D. Sagan, D. Abell, A. Coxe · first author · NAPAC 2025, Sacramento, CA · JACoW, pp. 65–67

A Julia package for the SciBmad accelerator-simulation project that gives one interface to physical constants, subatomic-particle properties, and isotope data from CODATA/NIST and the Particle Data Group. A single macro fixes the unit system and numeric type up front, keeping the code type-stable with core calls under a microsecond. I co-led its development and presented the poster.

Evaluating the Architectural Reasoning Capabilities of LLM Provers via the Obfuscated Natural Number Game

Lixing Li · sole author · Proceedings of the AAAI Symposium Series 8(1), 588–591 · published May 18, 2026

Do LLM theorem provers reason, or do they lean on familiar names? I introduce architectural reasoning and a 68-problem Lean 4 benchmark with six identifier-obfuscation levels, with proofs checked by the Lean compiler. All five evaluated models slowed down significantly under obfuscation, while their accuracy robustness differed.

System-Prompt Anchoring with Cross-Attention Layers

Lixing Li · sole author

Routes system prompts into frozen causal decoders through trainable cross-attention layers. Across a ten-placement study on Qwen2.5-1.5B and transfer to Llama-3.1-8B, the 8B model gains 7.4 points on IFEval and 16.3 points on SysBench over parameter-matched ParallelMLP adapters, with task-dependent benefits.

ReconSpan: Reconstruction-Guided Adaptive Latent Tokenization

Lixing Li · sole author

Compresses text into adaptive latent spans using backward reconstruction fidelity. One trained autoencoder supports average chunk lengths from 6.5 to 12.2 tokens, and at matched latent counts its boundaries preserve more source tokens in native reconstruction than equal-spacing, surprisal, or random boundaries. Readers recover topic information more reliably than exact details.

arXiv Sep 23, 2026 Preprint

NGN: Learning Neural Network Size as a Differentiable Count

Lixing Li · sole author

Learns a network's structural capacity as a differentiable count, jointly with its weights. The learned prefix can be deployed by hard truncation, usually with little change in performance. Controlled studies cover MLP, convolutional, graph, Transformer, Mamba, LoRA, adapter, depth, and structured-pruning settings.
